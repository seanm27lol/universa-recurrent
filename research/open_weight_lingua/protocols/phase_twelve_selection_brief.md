# Phase Twelve: does the late read take the most recent intact assignment? (exploratory)

**Status: FROZEN 2026-10-12 (UTC), before any Phase Twelve model forward.
Branch `phase-twelve-selection`, based on `main` at `40c7e78`. Exploratory,
on programs that have all been opened before. A confirmatory test would use
a third freshly generated split under its own frozen protocol.**

## The question

```text
x = 2              ← v2: the value two assignments back
y = 3
x = x + 9          ← v1: 11, the previous value
y = 5
x = 7              ← v0: 7, the final assignment (a literal) and the answer
What is x? Reply with only the integer.
```

Phases Ten and Eleven confirmed, on fresh programs, that degrading the
asked variable's final statement makes the model answer its **previous**
value ([report](../reports/phase_eleven_confirmatory.md)). A natural
reading is a selection rule: the late read takes the **most recent intact**
assignment of the asked variable.

That rule makes a further prediction. Degrading *both* the final and the
previous assignment should push the answer back once more, to v2.

## Design

- **Models, scoring and controls.** Gemma-3-12B and Qwen2.5-7B;
  teacher-forced digit accuracy; the bitwise no-op control; the Phase
  Eight–Eleven knockout hook and line assignment.
- **Program pool (all opened before).**
  - Phase Two's calibration and pilot prompts, from their saved token IDs.
  - Phase Nine's `fresh_2026_10` (256 groups) and Phase Eleven's
    `fresh_2026_10b` (512 groups). Both are regenerated by the code and
    verified against their recorded inventory hashes (`8e0d30ab…f963`,
    `67dc17bb…2a39`), then tokenized with each family's chat template.
- **Eligible prompts.** The asked variable has at least three assignments,
  its final statement is a literal, and v0 (the answer), v1 (the value
  before the final statement) and v2 (the value before the previous
  statement) are pairwise distinct. Expected: about 300 prompts.
- **Degrading a statement** means blocking its tokens, from layer 0, from
  every earlier program token, whichever variable's line it belongs to.
  This combines Phase Eleven's two blocks.

| Condition | Statements degraded |
|---|---|
| `baseline` | none |
| `final` | the final assignment |
| `final_and_previous` | the final and the previous assignment |
| `previous` | the previous assignment only (control) |

- **Measures, per condition.**
  - **says v0, v1, v2:** every digit of that value is the argmax when it is
    teacher-forced.
  - The log-probability of each value.
- **Cost.** 4 conditions × 3 teacher-forced sequences, plus the no-op
  control: 13 forwards per prompt.

## Predictions (recorded before any forward)

1. The no-op control is exact.
2. **`final`** reproduces Phase Eleven: accuracy drops by more than 25
   points, and v1 is the most common wrong answer.
3. **`final_and_previous`**: the rate of saying v2 exceeds its rate under
   `final` by more than 10 points, and v2 is said more often than v1. This
   is the most-recent-intact rule.
4. **`previous`** alone costs under 10 points, because the final literal
   still determines the answer.

## Budget, honesty, limits

- **Budget:** at most 1 GPU-hour. Each run starts only on an idle GPU;
  foreign processes are logged.
- **Limits.**
  - The degrading block is an off-distribution intervention. It is known
    to cause fallback (Phase Eleven), but not to remove information
    completely.
  - Only literal final statements are tested.
  - The pool reuses opened programs, so this is exploratory.
  - Two degraded statements may interact in ways beyond the selection
    rule.

**Code:** `src/open_weight_lingua/selection_knockout.py`, launched with
`scripts/run_selection_knockout.sh`.
